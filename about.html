What are we doing here?

Los Toros is not about deciding which movies are best. It’s about discovering which movies are worth coming back to—and why.

The Dennis

One member makes the case.

Any core member can nominate any film. One nomination puts it in The Dennis. A second moves it to the Queue.

The Queue

Two people get it in. More support moves it up.

Once a film is in the Queue, other members can support it too. The more Toros who want to see it, the higher it sits. The picker still makes the final call.

The Watch

In person or async.

Three core-member ratings make an official Los Toros result. Ratings stay personal until reveal.

The Reveal

Score first. Argue second.

Craft, Emotional Impact, Rewatchability and Overall. The scores give the conversation somewhere to start; they are not meant to end it.

The four numbers

Craft — how fully the film realizes its formal and technical ambitions.

Emotional Impact — what force it carries once it lands: pleasure, dread, grief, exhilaration, unease, laughter.

Rewatchability — not quality, but the desire and reward of returning.

Overall — the broadest judgment: taken as a whole, how highly do you rate the film?

What makes a film rewatchable?

Rewatchability is not quality. Overall already covers that. Rewatchability is closer to density plus desire: does the film contain more than one viewing can exhaust, and does returning feel like pleasure rather than homework?

A masterpiece can be something you never need to see again. A less imposing film can become part of your life because it keeps opening up—or simply because you want to spend time inside it again.

1–2 — Once was plenty, even if I admired it.

3–4 — I might revisit it in five or ten years, but there is no pull.

5–6 — Happy to rewatch if someone else puts it on.

7–8 — I will seek it out again on my own.

9–10 — Part of my life; I return to it the way I return to an album.

The craft of cinema

Craft is not a mood score. It asks how fully a film executes the disciplines that make cinema cinema. We still give Craft one number; these ten areas are the things to look at while deciding it.

1. Directing — the organizing intelligence of the film: performance, tone, blocking, rhythm, emphasis, point of view.

2. Screenplay — structure, scene construction and dialogue; especially the setups, echoes and reversals that become clearer once you know the whole.

3. Editing — rhythm, duration and juxtaposition. Great cutting shapes what we notice and what we feel without announcing itself.

4. Cinematography — camera, light and composition: not prettiness, but the precision of the image and what the frame makes possible.

5. Production Design — the material world of the film. Detail, space, objects and architecture can make a movie richer each time through.

6. Performance — choices in voice, gesture, timing and behavior that gain new meaning once the character’s full arc is known.

7. Sound Design — the acoustic world beyond dialogue and music; often one of the least noticed and most revealing layers on a second viewing.

8. Score / Music — the film’s musical architecture: theme, counterpoint, restraint, repetition and emotional memory.

9. Costume / Hair / Makeup — character and social information carried through appearance; often quietly tracking status, relationships and transformation.

10. Visual & Practical Effects — effects that endure as craft rather than merely as surprise. Spectacle fades; convincing physical and visual design tends to hold.

1–2 — Fundamentally weak or incoherent execution.

3–4 — Noticeably uneven; some strong work, but major elements fail.

5–6 — Competent to solid professional filmmaking.

7–8 — Distinctive, accomplished work with real formal control.

9–10 — Exceptional craft; the filmmaking itself becomes a reason to return.

The recurring connection is density. Direction, writing, design, editing, performance and sound can all hide information in plain sight. Those are often the qualities that make a second viewing feel different from the first.

Canon is not “best.”

Canon is our record of films that work on both axes that matter here: Overall and Rewatchability. An official screening enters Canon only when both group averages are at least 7.0.

That distinction matters. A film can be revered, beautifully made and highly rated while still falling outside Canon because the group does not particularly want to return to it.

Canon gate: Overall ≥ 7.0 and Rewatchability ≥ 7.0, based on an official screening with at least three core-member ratings.
