How the machine works

The rules are simple on purpose. Enough structure to make the conversation sharper; not enough to turn movie night into municipal government.

1. The Dennis

One core-member nomination. That is all it takes.

A film in The Dennis has one person saying, “we should come back to this.” No score threshold. No committee. No requirement that anybody else agree yet.

2. The Queue

A second core member moves the film into the Queue.

After that, other Toros do not “nominate” it again — they support it. Three supporters outrank two; four outrank three. Support creates momentum, not an automatic selection. The person whose turn it is to pick still chooses from the eligible Queue.

3. Watching is not nominating

Anybody can watch and rate any movie at any time. Two Toros can happen to watch the same non-Queue movie. A Toro can watch with a friend. Guests can join a Queue-film watch.

Those ratings are real, but watching a film does not place it in The Dennis or the Queue. Nomination is a separate act: “I want the club to consider this for an official rewatch.”

4. Blind scoring and the reveal

Scores are hidden until you submit yours. You have to give to get.

For a shared casual watch, the reveal opens as soon as two people have locked scores. Members and guests can compare category by category. Those ratings remain outside official Los Toros Canon unless the watch becomes an official club screening.

5. What counts as Watched?

On the Los Toros Board, Watched means an official Los Toros result: at least three core-member ratings attached to that screening.

Watched films are ranked by the average of all four scoring categories:

(Craft + Emotional Impact + Rewatchability + Overall) ÷ 4

That combined score is for ranking. It is not the Canon test.

6. What makes Canon?

Canon asks a narrower question: did the film score highly and make us want to return?

Overall average ≥ 7.0
Rewatchability average ≥ 7.0

Both must clear. Craft and Emotional Impact still matter to the film's full score, but they do not substitute for the two Canon gates.

7. Highest Rated is different

Highest Rated ranks member-rated films by the four-category combined score, whether or not they qualify for Canon.

This preserves an important distinction: a film can be one of the most admired things we have watched and still not be something we particularly want to revisit.

8. Repeat watches

A new watch can produce a new rating. Old ratings stay put. The record should show how somebody's response changed over time rather than quietly rewriting what they thought before.

Baseball version: anybody can enjoy the game. One person may know OPS+, defensive shifts and why the manager pulled the starter in the sixth; another may simply know they had a hell of a night. Both belong in the stands. The scoring system is there for people who want to see more of the machinery — not as an entrance exam.
