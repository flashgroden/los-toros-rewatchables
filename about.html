Why Los Toros?

Because “great movie” and “movie I want to live with” are not the same thing.

Los Toros tries to preserve both judgments. A film can be one of the greatest things you have ever seen and still be something you rarely want to revisit. Another can be imperfect and become part of your life because you keep returning to it.

The scores are not intended to settle anything. They make those differences visible — between execution, experience, greatness and the desire to return.

The Dennis

One person raises a hand.

A nomination is not a verdict. It is simply one Toro saying: this deserves another look. One nomination puts the film in The Dennis; a second moves it into the Queue.

The Queue

Interest becomes momentum.

Once two members want the film back, it is eligible. More members can support it, which moves it higher. The picker still has judgment.

The Watch

Fans and newcomers share the same game.

A formalist and a first-time viewer can have equally valid nights at the movies; one may simply notice more of the machinery.

The Reveal

Score first. Talk second.

Blind scoring protects the first reaction from the room. Once scores are locked, the differences become material for discussion rather than social pressure.

The four numbers

Craft — how fully the film realizes its formal and technical ambitions.

Emotional Impact — what force it carries once it lands: pleasure, dread, grief, exhilaration, unease, laughter.

Rewatchability — not quality, but the desire and reward of returning.

Overall — greatness. Where does this film belong among films, all things considered? This is not simply “how much did I enjoy it tonight?”

Two useful lists come out of the same scorecard:

Greats are ranked primarily by Overall.

Rewatchables are ranked primarily by Rewatchability.

Canon is where the two overlap.

What makes a film rewatchable?

Rewatchability is not quality. Overall measures greatness. Rewatchability is closer to density plus desire: does the film contain more than one viewing can exhaust, and does returning feel like pleasure rather than homework?

A masterpiece can be something you never need to see again. A lesser film can become part of your life because it keeps opening up — or because you simply want to spend time inside it again.

1–2 — Once was plenty, even if I admired it.

3–4 — I might revisit it in five or ten years, but there is no pull.

5–6 — Happy to rewatch if someone else puts it on.

7–8 — I will seek it out again on my own.

9–10 — Part of my life; I return to it the way I return to an album.

The craft of cinema

Craft is not “did it look good?” It asks how fully the movie uses the disciplines available to it. We still give one Craft score; these are the lenses behind it.

1. Directing — the organizing intelligence: performance, tone, blocking, rhythm, emphasis, point of view.

2. Screenplay — structure, scene construction and dialogue; especially the setups, echoes and reversals that become clearer once you know the whole.

3. Editing — rhythm, duration and juxtaposition.

4. Cinematography — camera, light and composition: not prettiness, but precision and meaning inside the frame.

5. Production Design — the physical world of the movie.

6. Performance — voice, gesture, timing and behavior.

7. Sound Design — the acoustic world beyond dialogue and music.

8. Score / Music — the musical architecture.

9. Costume / Hair / Makeup — character and social information carried through appearance.

10. Visual & Practical Effects — effects that endure as craft rather than merely as surprise.

1–2 — Fundamentally weak or incoherent execution.

3–4 — Noticeably uneven; some strong work, but major elements fail.

5–6 — Competent to solid professional filmmaking.

7–8 — Distinctive, accomplished work with real formal control.

9–10 — Exceptional craft; the filmmaking itself becomes a reason to return.

Canon is where greatness meets return.

Club Canon is our record of official Los Toros screenings that succeed on both axes: Overall and Rewatchability. Both group averages must clear 7.0.

Each critic can also have a Personal Canon using the same idea: their current Overall score and current Rewatchability score both clear 7.0. That personal Canon can include solo watches, family watches and side watches; it does not require a club screening.

Club Canon gate: group Overall ≥ 7.0 and group Rewatchability ≥ 7.0, with at least three core-member ratings.

Personal Canon gate: personal Overall ≥ 7.0 and personal Rewatchability ≥ 7.0.
