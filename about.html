Why Los Toros?

Because “great movie” is usually where the interesting conversation stops.

Los Toros starts somewhere else: what makes a film worth returning to? Not merely admired, not merely important, not merely canonical in the capital-C sense. Returned to. Lived with. Argued over again after the plot is no longer the point.

The scores are not intended to settle anything. They make disagreement visible. A film can be impeccably made and emotionally remote. It can be messy and irresistible. It can be a masterpiece nobody wants to revisit. Those differences are the conversation.

The Dennis

One person raises a hand.

A nomination is not a verdict. It is simply one Toro saying: this deserves another look. One nomination puts the film in The Dennis; a second moves it into the Queue.

The Queue

Interest becomes momentum.

Once two members want the film back, it is eligible. More members can support it, which moves it higher. Think of it less like a ballot and more like a pennant race: support matters, but the picker still has judgment.

The Watch

Fans and newcomers share the same game.

You do not need to know the infield-fly rule to love baseball. The same goes here. A formalist and a first-time viewer can have equally valid nights at the movies; one may simply notice more of the machinery.

The Reveal

Score first. Talk second.

Blind scoring protects the first reaction from the room. Once scores are locked, the differences become material for discussion rather than social pressure.

The four numbers

Craft — how fully the film realizes its formal and technical ambitions.

Emotional Impact — what force it carries once it lands: pleasure, dread, grief, exhilaration, unease, laughter.

Rewatchability — not quality, but the desire and reward of returning.

Overall — the broadest judgment: taken as a whole, how highly do you rate the film?

What makes a film rewatchable?

Rewatchability is not quality. Overall already covers that. Rewatchability is closer to density plus desire: does the film contain more than one viewing can exhaust, and does returning feel like pleasure rather than homework?

A masterpiece can be something you never need to see again. A lesser film can become part of your life because it keeps opening up — or because you simply want to spend time inside it again.

1–2 — Once was plenty, even if I admired it.

3–4 — I might revisit it in five or ten years, but there is no pull.

5–6 — Happy to rewatch if someone else puts it on.

7–8 — I will seek it out again on my own.

9–10 — Part of my life; I return to it the way I return to an album.

The craft of cinema

Craft is not “did it look good?” It asks how fully the movie uses the disciplines available to it. We still give one Craft score; these are the lenses behind it.

1. Directing — the organizing intelligence: performance, tone, blocking, rhythm, emphasis, point of view.

2. Screenplay — structure, scene construction and dialogue; especially the setups, echoes and reversals that become clearer once you know the whole.

3. Editing — rhythm, duration and juxtaposition. Great cutting shapes what we notice and feel without announcing itself.

4. Cinematography — camera, light and composition: not prettiness, but precision and meaning inside the frame.

5. Production Design — the physical world of the movie. Space, objects, texture and detail often become richer on return.

6. Performance — voice, gesture, timing and behavior that can acquire new meaning once the character’s full arc is known.

7. Sound Design — the acoustic world beyond dialogue and music; often the least noticed layer on first viewing and one of the richest on the second.

8. Score / Music — the musical architecture: theme, counterpoint, restraint, repetition and emotional memory.

9. Costume / Hair / Makeup — character and social information carried through appearance; status, relationships, transformation.

10. Visual & Practical Effects — effects that endure as craft rather than merely as surprise. Spectacle fades; convincing design tends to hold.

1–2 — Fundamentally weak or incoherent execution.

3–4 — Noticeably uneven; some strong work, but major elements fail.

5–6 — Competent to solid professional filmmaking.

7–8 — Distinctive, accomplished work with real formal control.

9–10 — Exceptional craft; the filmmaking itself becomes a reason to return.

The throughline is density. Direction, writing, design, editing, performance and sound can all hide information in plain sight. Those are often the qualities that make a second viewing feel different from the first.

Canon is not “best.”

Canon is our record of official Los Toros screenings that succeed on both axes that define the club: Overall and Rewatchability. Both group averages must clear 7.0.

That distinction is deliberate. Reverence is not the same thing as return. A film may be formidable, beautiful, historically essential — and still leave the group with no appetite to see it again.

Canon gate: Overall ≥ 7.0 and Rewatchability ≥ 7.0, based on an official screening with at least three core-member ratings.
